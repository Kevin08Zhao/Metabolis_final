why_failures_before_assist_equals_the_M5_threshold: |
  It is set to 2, the same value as minigames.failure.failures_before_ease.
  Table M5 of docs/MINIGAME_SPEC.md already eases the difficulty tier after a
  streak of failures, and T-33a offers assist mode after a streak of failures.
  Two mechanisms firing at two different counts would mean two unexplained
  moments in a row where the game quietly gets easier, so the two are held
  together and the validation row records that they are meant to stay equal.
  They remain separate mechanisms: the eased tier is the minigame runtime's own
  difficulty change under T-19a, and assist mode is this system publishing three
  parameters. Neither reads the other.

table_H1_the_three_scopes: |
  | scope_id | screened | assist allowed | resets on |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | minigame | no | yes | minigame_entered |
  | build_decision | yes | no | build_options_presented |
  | operation_decision | yes | no | phase_changed reaching step operation_decision |

  `screened` is what makes a scope's wording pass the banned-term list.
  `assist_allowed` is what makes report_failure accept a call. Both are read by
  the logic; neither scope is named in a function body.

  The operation scope resets on a step change rather than on
  resource_priority_changed, which is the event that otherwise marks its
  opening. That event fires on every drag of the allocation, so a player who had
  climbed to level three would be dropped back to level one by nudging the
  slider. The acceptance run emits two drags and confirms the level holds.
table_H3_the_nine_phrasing_templates: |
  One template per level per scope. Braced names are the substitutions a caller
  fills; nothing else is replaced. Printed by the driver from the same data the
  code hands out.

  | scope | level | level_id | template |
  |---|---:|---|---|
  | minigame | 1 | hint_observe | `Current goal: {goal}. You have {remaining} of {limit} left.` |
  | minigame | 2 | hint_compare | `The trouble is in {area}: {measure} is at {value}, and the goal needs {required}.` |
  | minigame | 3 | hint_consequence | `Next step: {action} at {location}, within {window}.` |
  | build_decision | 1 | hint_observe | `Current goal: settle {decision}. Each card shows {dimension_count} measured values and its resource cost.` |
  | build_decision | 2 | hint_compare | `The cards differ along {dimension}: one reads {value_a} {unit} and the other {value_b} {unit}. A higher reading means {high_meaning}; a lower one means {low_meaning}.` |
  | build_decision | 3 | hint_consequence | `A choice can be made here. {dimension_1} measures {meaning_1}; {dimension_2} measures {meaning_2}; {dimension_3} measures {meaning_3}. The choice is yours to make.` |
  | operation_decision | 1 | hint_observe | `Current goal: settle {decision}. The allocation has to total {required_total}, and it now totals {current_total}.` |
  | operation_decision | 2 | hint_compare | `The trouble is in {area}: {measure} reads {value} {unit}. Moving the allocation toward {direction_a} pulls it one way and toward {direction_b} the other.` |
  | operation_decision | 3 | hint_consequence | `A choice can be made here. {dimension_1} measures {meaning_1}; {dimension_2} measures {meaning_2}; {dimension_3} measures {meaning_3}. The choice is yours to make.` |

  Level three is the only row that differs in kind between the scopes. The
  minigame has a correct next operation, so it shows one. The two decisions do
  not: table D11 of docs/BUILD_DECISION_SPEC.md requires that neither candidate
  dominate, so a hint naming a next step there would be inventing a preference
  the design deliberately does not have. It states that a choice exists and what
  the three dimensions measure, and stops.
table_H2_the_banned_term_list: |
  Terms that may not appear in a hint at the build decision or the operation
  decision. Twenty-seven in all. The repository writes its copy in English, as
  the scope note of docs/UI_COPY.md records, so each term is listed in the
  English form the copy would actually use.

  | position | term the prompt requires | forms listed |
  |---:|---|---|
  | 1 | first required term | `optimal`, `best`, `ideal` |
  | 2 | second required term | `recommend`, `recommended`, `recommendation` |
  | 3 | third required term | `should choose`, `should pick`, `should select` |
  | 4 | fourth required term | `better`, `superior`, `worse`, `worst` |
  | 5 | fifth required term | `suggest`, `suggested`, `suggestion`, `advise`, `advice` |

  From the forbidden column of table D7 of docs/BUILD_DECISION_SPEC.md:
  `ranking`, `ranked`, `top choice`, `correct option`, `right option`,
  `wrong option`, `prevents failure`, `guarantees success`,
  `avoid this option`.

  Matching strips comments and respects word boundaries. Both rules exist
  because a plain substring search flags a file for documenting the rule it
  obeys; that has now happened twice in this account's work, in T-25 and T-32.
  The screen is applied twice: once at startup over every screened template, and
  again on every line handed out, so a caller cannot smuggle a term in through a
  substitution.
